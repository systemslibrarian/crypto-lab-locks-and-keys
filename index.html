WHY IT MATTERS

Public-key cryptography uses two separate, mathematically related keys: a public key you share and a private key you keep secret.

Why RSA here, and what this demo is not

RSA-OAEP lets you encrypt a short note directly with a public key. Modern protocols often use public-key operations to establish a shared key, then encrypt messages with symmetric cryptography. Iron Letter compares the two directly, and HPKE Envelope shows a hybrid encryption construction.

This teaching demo uses real browser cryptography. It makes the private key exportable so Step 4 can reuse the RSA pair for signing. Production systems commonly restrict key export and use separate pairs for encryption and signing.

For the mathematics behind RSA, visit Educational RSA, for a step-by-step explanation.

Why the counter can run ahead of what you typed

The limit counts UTF-8 bytes, not characters. ASCII letters use one byte; accented letters and emoji can use several.
